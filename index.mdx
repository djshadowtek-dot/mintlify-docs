Artificial Intelligence Researching starts with a clear question and evidence you can inspect. Use this hub to plan GPT-assisted research workflows for The-Awesome.ai, beginning with research discovery, experiment planning, and model evaluation.

<Note>
  These are proposed workflows, not connected services or running experiments. Available capabilities depend on your model, plan, and approved tools. No automation or live telemetry is configured by this page.
</Note>

## Research Assistant

Use GPT to summarize supplied papers, compare methods, and identify questions worth investigating. Ask it to distinguish source-supported findings from interpretations and untested hypotheses.

**Starter prompt:** “Compare these papers on retrieval-augmented generation. For each, list the research question, dataset, baseline, findings, limitations, and supporting passages. Flag missing information rather than guessing.”

Verify every citation against the original source before including it in your research notes.

## Experiment Planner

Translate a research question into a testable plan. Record the hypothesis, dataset selection, baseline, metrics, controls, and stopping criteria before running an experiment.

**Starter prompt:** “Design a comparison between a retrieval-assisted answer system and a no-retrieval baseline using the same questions. Specify how to assess factual support, answer quality, latency, and cost without inventing results.”

Keep model versions, prompts, configuration, and dataset versions with each run.

## Evaluation Lab

Create a fixed evaluation set and a scoring rubric before comparing models. Separate automated checks from human judgments, and record failures as well as successful answers.

**Starter prompt:** “Draft a rubric for source-grounded answers covering citation accuracy, unsupported claims, completeness, and appropriate uncertainty. Include human-review instructions and examples of failure categories.”

Report accuracy, latency, and cost only when you have measured them. Label simulated data explicitly.

## Coding Assistant

Use GPT to explain code, draft prototypes, write tests, and investigate errors from supplied logs. Review generated code before running it, especially when it accesses files, networks, or external services.

**Starter prompt:** “Review this evaluation script. Explain its assumptions, propose tests for missing data and invalid responses, and identify changes that could alter the reported scores.”

## Data Analyst

Explore uploaded datasets, check missing values, and propose visualizations. Where code execution is available, inspect the analysis code and confirm that calculations match your definitions.

**Starter prompt:** “Inspect this experiment-results dataset. Identify missing values, summarize results by model, and explain which comparisons the sample size does not support.”

Use de-identified data and document statistical assumptions.

## Knowledge Assistant

Plan an assistant that answers from approved research documents. Require source references and an explicit response when the documents do not contain an answer.

**Starter prompt:** “Answer using only the supplied research notes. Cite the relevant section for each claim. If the evidence is missing or contradictory, explain the limitation.”

A searchable document collection requires an appropriate retrieval setup; this page does not connect one.

## Multimodal Research

Where supported, use image input to inspect diagrams, screenshots, or scanned material, and audio tools to work with recordings. Verify extracted labels, equations, and transcripts manually.

**Starter prompt:** “Explain this architecture diagram. Separate visible labels from inferred relationships, and flag text or connections that are unclear.”

Do not treat a visual interpretation as a measurement or proof.

## Writing Studio

Turn verified notes into research summaries, documentation, presentation outlines, or draft reports. Preserve citations and uncertainty instead of turning tentative findings into definitive claims.

**Starter prompt:** “Draft a technical summary from these verified notes. Include methods, findings, limitations, and open questions. Do not add numerical results or references.”

## Learning Tutor

Build lessons and exercises around your current research topic. Ask for explanations at different levels, then test understanding with worked problems and feedback.

**Starter prompt:** “Teach me how precision and recall apply to document retrieval. Start with a practical example, give me an exercise, and explain the tradeoffs after I answer.”

## Workflow Automation

Identify repeatable tasks such as drafting paper summaries or preparing evaluation reports. Connect only approved tools, use minimal permissions, and require human approval before publishing, modifying data, or taking consequential actions.

**Starter prompt:** “Outline a workflow that summarizes newly approved papers into draft research notes. Include source checks, duplicate detection, human review, and an audit record. Do not publish automatically.”

## First Research Cycle

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose One Question">
    Select a narrow research question and collect the sources you are authorized to use.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write the Experiment Plan">
    Define the hypothesis, baseline, evaluation set, scoring rubric, and reproducibility details.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Run and Record Results">
    Execute the experiment separately, retain the actual outputs, and record the configuration and measurements.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review Before Sharing">
    Verify citations, inspect errors, document limitations, and approve the final research summary.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  Do not submit credentials, confidential documents, or personal data unless your organization's policies and the selected service's data controls permit it. GPT output can be incorrect and requires verification.
</Warning>
